# Frontier Map — Representaciones de docentes de nivel primario sobre la IA generativa (UNSAM)

Full discussion: `quality_reports/lit_review_ia-generativa-docentes-primaria.md`, Section 4.

## What's been done

1. A large, fast-growing international literature (2022–2026) on teacher perceptions/acceptance of generative AI, dominated by individual technology-acceptance frameworks (TAM, UTAUT, TPACK), higher-education and pre-service-teacher contexts, and largely quantitative/survey methods.
2. A small but growing number of K-12/elementary-specific empirical studies (Calleja & Camilleri 2025, Malta; Han et al. 2024, US; Yu, Lee & Kim 2025, US) that reach the primary level but use acceptance/HCI frames rather than structural Social Representations Theory, and typically focus on one bounded task (literacy, math lesson planning) rather than the full range of teaching tasks.
3. An established Latin American tradition of studying teachers' social representations of ICT/ed-tech (Padilla & Silva 2017; Ramos Ramón & Frías López 2019; Clavijo Izquierdo 2025 review) — but pre-dating or not specific to generative AI, and concentrated in secondary/higher-ed/b-learning contexts, not primary.
4. Exactly three Latin American studies combine Social Representations Theory explicitly with generative AI: Arigón (Argentina, secondary level), the CFE Uruguay thesis (primary level, framing not confirmed as structural), and Carvalho & Corrallo (Brazil, vocational education, materials-production focus only).

## The gap

No identified study combines all of: (a) structural Social Representations Theory (núcleo central/periférico, Moscovici-Jodelet-Abric), (b) in-service primary-level teachers, (c) generative AI specifically, (d) dual coverage of classroom use and teaching-task use (planning, grading, feedback, materials), (e) Argentine context. This thesis sits in that intersection.
