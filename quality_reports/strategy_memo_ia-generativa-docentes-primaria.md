### Research Question (verbatim from the real thesis plan — not reconstructed/invented)

**Principal:** "¿Cuáles son las representaciones de los docentes de escuelas primarias de la Ciudad Autónoma de Buenos Aires respecto al potencial de la IA generativa para promover un aprendizaje significativo en sus estudiantes a partir de propuestas didácticas creadas con algoritmos generativos?"

**Sub-preguntas:**
1. ¿Cómo se relacionan las competencias digitales de los docentes con sus representaciones sobre los algoritmos generativos?
2. ¿De qué manera los conocimientos previos sobre inteligencia artificial influyen en las representaciones que construyen los docentes sobre el uso de algoritmos generativos?
3. ¿Qué preocupaciones manifiestan los docentes respecto al uso de algoritmos generativos en el diseño de sus propuestas didácticas?

**Objetivo general:** Explorar las representaciones de los docentes de escuelas primarias de la CABA sobre el uso de algoritmos generativos en el diseño de propuestas didácticas que promuevan aprendizajes significativos, considerando sus competencias digitales, conocimientos previos y preocupaciones asociadas.

**Objetivos específicos (verbatim):**
1. Identificar las competencias digitales de los docentes de escuelas primarias de CABA en relación con el uso de herramientas tecnológicas para el diseño de propuestas didácticas orientadas al aprendizaje significativo.
2. Analizar los conocimientos previos que poseen los docentes sobre algoritmos generativos e inteligencia artificial, y su relación con sus representaciones sobre el potencial de estas herramientas para promover aprendizajes significativos.
3. Describir las preocupaciones y expectativas que manifiestan los docentes respecto al uso de algoritmos generativos para diseñar propuestas didácticas que favorezcan el aprendizaje significativo.
4. Explorar cómo los docentes conciben la relación entre el uso de algoritmos generativos y la promoción de aprendizajes significativos en sus propuestas didácticas.
5. Caracterizar los tipos de usos que los docentes consideran posibles y deseables para los algoritmos generativos en el diseño de propuestas didácticas que potencien el aprendizaje significativo de sus estudiantes.

**Critical correction confirmed against the primary source:** Nowhere in the thesis plan is there an "aula" (in-classroom, student-facing) vs. "tareas_docentes" (teacher's own preparation work) domain split. The object throughout is squarely **the teacher's own representation of IAG's potential for designing didactic proposals that foster meaningful learning** — i.e., the teacher's planning/preparation work (which maps almost exactly onto interview Protocol Block 6, "usos proyectados de IA generativa en propuestas didácticas"). The aula/tareas split in the superseded memo was an invention not grounded in either the thesis plan or the protocol. **This memo drops it entirely** (see §6.2 below).

## 1. Analytic Object

**What exactly is being reconstructed?** The representation that primary-level teachers in CABA construct of **generative AI's potential to foster meaningful learning ("aprendizaje significativo," Ausubel) via didactic proposals they themselves design** — i.e., the teacher's own sense-making about a specific pedagogical-design use of IAG, not a generic "attitude toward AI" and not a separate "classroom use vs. teacher-task use" duality.

This single, unified object is analyzed along **four axes drawn directly from the thesis plan's own objetivos específicos** (not invented, not an aula/tareas split):

| Axis | Source (objetivo específico) | Protocol block(s) most relevant |
|---|---|---|
| A. Competencias digitales y su relación con las representaciones sobre IAG | OE1 | Bloque 2 (competencias digitales/TIC); Bloque 1 (perfil) |
| B. Conocimientos previos sobre IA/algoritmos generativos y su relación con las representaciones sobre su potencial | OE2 | Bloque 3 (conocimientos previos), especialmente el ancla de objetivación en Bloque 3-Q1 (ver §2.4) |
| C. Preocupaciones y expectativas sobre el uso de IAG en el diseño de propuestas didácticas | OE3 | Bloque 4 (preocupaciones/expectativas) |
| D. Concepción de la relación IAG–aprendizaje significativo, y tipos de usos posibles/deseables en el diseño de propuestas didácticas | OE4, OE5 | Bloque 5 (IAG y aprendizaje significativo); Bloque 6 (usos proyectados en propuestas didácticas) |

Cutting across all four axes: the **imaginarios sociotécnicos dimension** (does the teacher's discourse echo the official/national imaginary — Guía 2025, Educ.ar — or depart from it in an experientially-grounded way?) and the **classic SRT process pair** (objetivación: what concrete image does the teacher reach for when naming/defining IAG; anclaje: what pre-existing frame — prior EdTech, a colleague, a fear, a hope — does the teacher integrate IAG into?).

**Population:** Docentes de nivel primario en ejercicio en escuelas de CABA a las que la investigadora tuvo acceso — explicitly not claimed as representative of "docentes de nivel primario" in general (see §4, §6.1).

**Unit of analysis:** El discurso de cada docente entrevistado/a (n=14-15), segmentado en unidades de sentido (fragmentos de entrevista), codificadas según el esquema de §2, condicionado explícitamente por qué bloques del protocolo fueron efectivamente administrados a cada participante (see §6.1).

**What this design explicitly does NOT attempt:** A structural (Abric) reconstruction of núcleo central/periférico; a parallel, exhaustively-coded documentary analysis of the Guía 2025 and Educ.ar corpus as an independent object of study; a classroom-observation or student-facing-use component (none of the 7 protocol blocks elicit this — Block 6 is squarely about the teacher's own planning/preparation, not what happens with students present).

## 2. Coding / Analysis Plan (Grounded Theory)

### 2.1 Deductive seed categories — one family per theoretical núcleo

Grounded Theory here is **not** "pure" Glaser/Strauss ground-zero induction. Per the thesis plan's own Estrategia metodológica and the domain-profile, four theoretical núcleos seed the initial codebook. Genuine emergence still matters (§2.3) — seeding is a starting scaffold, not a cage.

| Núcleo | Anchor | Seed code family | Example codes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Representaciones sociales clásicas | Moscovici (1986); Jodelet (1991, 2011) | `objetivación`, `anclaje` | `imagen_concreta_IAG` (what concrete image/metaphor names IAG), `anclaje_tecnologia_previa` (compared to a prior EdTech, e.g., calculadora, Google, corrector ortográfico), `anclaje_figura_humana` (compared to an assistant, a colleague, a student, a robot) |
| 2. Aprendizaje significativo | Ausubel (1963) | `aprendizaje_significativo` | `potencia_integracion_sustantiva` (teacher sees IAG as helping connect new content to students' prior knowledge), `limita_integracion_sustantiva` (teacher sees IAG as producing arbitrary/rote integration, or bypassing genuine understanding), `adaptacion_saberes_previos` (teacher describes/imagines IAG adapting to specific students' prior knowledge) |
| 3. Alfabetización digital docente | Lankshear & Knobel (2008); Dussel (2009) | `alfabetizacion_digital` | `competencia_tecnica_declarada`, `competencia_critica_reflexiva` (goes beyond tool operation to critical/reflective stance), `relacion_no_lineal_competencia_representacion` (explicit codeing rule below) |
| 4. Imaginarios sociotécnicos (activo) | Jasanoff & Kim (2015); Saura, Lima & Arguelho (2024) | `imaginario_sociotecnico` | `eco_discurso_institucional`, `voz_experiencial_propia` (see §2.4 for full mechanics) |

**Explicit non-causal coding rule for Núcleo 3 (per domain-profile.md field convention):** Do not code a "mayor competencia digital → representación más favorable" pattern as a default assumption. The code `relacion_no_lineal_competencia_representacion` exists precisely to flag and report cases where high digital competence co-occurs with strong reservations, or low competence co-occurs with enthusiasm — these are expected, reportable patterns, not noise.

### 2.2 Inductive / open coding

Standard GT open coding runs in parallel with the deductive pass, not after it:

1. **Codificación abierta línea por línea** on the first 3-4 fully available interviews, without restricting to the seed families in §2.1 — name whatever the data suggests.
2. **Comparación constante:** every new segment is compared against previously coded segments and against emerging categories, not just tallied. Categories are actively revised (merged, split, renamed) as more interviews are coded, not fixed after the first pass.
3. **Memos analíticos:** dated, running memos on category development, decisions to merge/split, and surprises — this is both a GT method requirement and the Dependability/Confirmability audit trail (§3).
4. **Categorías axiales:** once open codes stabilize, group them into axial categories that relate deductive-seed and inductive-emergent codes to each other (e.g., how does `imagen_concreta_IAG` [objetivación] relate to `potencia_integracion_sustantiva` [Ausubel]? Does a teacher who objectifies IAG as "una herramienta como el diccionario" tend to see limited potential for meaningful learning, or does the metaphor not predict the pedagogical judgment at all?).
5. **Categoría central / teoría sustantiva:** GT's final integrative step — articulate a substantive account of how these axes relate for this population, not a mechanical list of frequencies.

### 2.3 Theoretical sensitivity vs. forcing — the central GT risk, addressed explicitly

**The risk:** with four deductive núcleos already specified before coding begins, there is a real danger that the codebook becomes a checklist applied *to* the data rather than a sensitizing framework that stays open to what the data actually says (Glaser's classic critique of "forcing" vs. genuine "emergence").

**Concrete mitigations, not just a disclaimer:**
- Every deductive code in §2.1 must have **at least one open/inductive code that could contradict or complicate it**, actively sought, not just permitted. E.g., if `potencia_integracion_sustantiva` is coded, actively search for and code counter-instances even within the same participant's transcript.
- **Track code provenance explicitly**: every code in the codebook is tagged `[deductivo]` or `[inductivo]` with the date it entered the codebook — this is a literal, auditable answer to the referee question "¿qué categorías fueron a priori y cuáles emergieron?" (domain-profile.md, Field-Specific Referee Concerns).
- If, after coding 6-8 interviews, an entire deductive núcleo (e.g., Núcleo 3, alfabetización digital) generates almost no distinct data beyond a single code with no elaboration, **report this explicitly as a finding** ("este eje aparece subdesarrollado en el corpus, posiblemente porque el protocolo no lo indaga en profundidad más allá del Bloque 2") rather than padding the write-up to make all four núcleos appear equally rich.
- Theoretical sensitivity is periodically checked via peer debriefing (§3) — a second reader flags places where the analysis looks like a template being filled in rather than an interpretation grounded in the specific words used.

### 2.4 Mechanics of "imaginarios sociotécnicos como dimensión analítica activa"

This is the operational core of the researcher's own resolved methodological question (backdrop vs. active dimension → she chose active). The mechanics, step by step:

**Step 1 — Build a short marker list from the light reference corpus (not a parallel coded corpus).**
Read the Guía 2025 (Programa PaideIA) and the Educ.ar 2025 survey report **once, closely, but not line-by-line coded**, extracting a short list (10-20 items) of recurring discursive markers characteristic of the official/national imaginary — e.g., phrases and framings such as "aprendizaje personalizado," "acompañamiento docente" (IA as support, not replacement), "uso responsable/ético," "necesidad de formación/capacitación docente," "brecha de formación" (echoing Educ.ar's own finding that most teachers lack specialized training), "eficiencia," "integración progresiva," "herramienta al servicio de," etc. This list is a **working instrument**, refined iteratively as coding proceeds (a teacher's phrase may reveal a marker not initially noticed in the light reading) — document each addition with its source.

**Step 2 — Code interview segments against two primary categories (per domain-profile.md, kept as the operational minimum):**
- `eco_discurso_institucional`: segment closely echoes/reproduces a marker from Step 1's list, explicitly or in close paraphrase — including direct references to capacitaciones, currículum oficial, or "lo que dicen que hay que hacer."
- `voz_experiencial_propia`: segment grounds the claim in a concrete episode, idiosyncratic metaphor, or personal reasoning not traceable to the reference markers — the teacher's own working-through of the idea, not a repetition of circulating discourse.

**Step 2b — Optional inductive refinement (not mandatory, document if it emerges):** Saura et al. (2024) themselves distinguish three imaginary registers — global (organismos internacionales), nacional (gobiernos), mercantilista (corporaciones/EdTech). The light reference corpus (Guía, Educ.ar) captures only the **nacional** register. If, during open coding, segments appear that echo commercial/media discourse instead (e.g., tech-company marketing language, viral claims about AI "replacing" teachers, hype language traceable to journalism rather than the Guía/Educ.ar) — these should NOT be miscoded as `voz_experiencial_propia` simply because they don't match the national-register marker list. Recommend adding a third code, `eco_imaginario_mercantil_mediatico`, if and only if such segments emerge with enough frequency to warrant it — this is a genuinely inductive addition, consistent with GT method, not a mandatory a priori category (the domain-profile only mandates the two-way contrast; this extension is this memo's judgment call, offered because Saura et al.'s own tripartite framework is explicitly adopted in the thesis plan's marco teórico and it would be a missed opportunity for theoretical sensitivity not to leave room for it).

**Step 3 — Analyze, don't just tally.** The reportable question is not "what % of segments are institutional echo," but: **where and how do teachers' testimonies echo the official imaginary, and where and how do they depart from it — and does this differ by axis (A-D, §1) or by teacher profile (§4)?** E.g., a teacher might echo institutional language almost verbatim when discussing "preocupaciones" (Axis C — perhaps because that's what capacitaciones emphasize) while showing strong experiential voice when discussing "usos proyectados" (Axis D — because that's where their own concrete planning practice lives, something no guía can script for them).

**Step 4 — Explicit guardrail against over-inference (the researcher's own stated risk, and the strongest referee objection — see §7, objection 5):** This procedure identifies **echoes of, and departures from, a specific national-imaginary marker list, within a 14-15-interview corpus** — it does **not** constitute a full sociotechnical-imaginaries analysis of the macro-level Argentine policy discourse, and does not claim to characterize "the" national imaginary independently (that would require the exhaustive documentary analysis this design deliberately does not undertake, per the researcher's own scope choice). Every reported claim about imaginaries must be phrased at the level of "in this teacher's testimony, X echoes/departs from marker Y in the reference corpus" — never "the national imaginary is characterized by X" as a freestanding claim derived from the interviews alone.

### 2.5 The objetivación anchor — Bloque 3, Pregunta 1

**"¿Qué sabés sobre la Inteligencia Artificial Generativa? ¿Si tuvieras que explicarle a alguien qué es la IAG cómo se lo explicarías?"**

This is the closest thing to a spontaneous-elicitation moment in the entire protocol — no other question asks the teacher to produce a first-person definition/explanation from scratch. Treat it as a **privileged data point for objetivación analysis**: code explicitly and separately what concrete image, metaphor, or comparison each teacher reaches for *first*, before any subsequent question shapes their framing. Report this as its own small table in the eventual paper (one row per participant: first spontaneous image/metaphor used) — this single question is analytically worth more per-word than most others precisely because it is the one moment the protocol elicits unprompted objetivación.

## 3. Rigor Criteria (Lincoln & Guba, operationalized for this design)

### Credibility
- **Statement:** Los hallazgos reflejan de manera creíble el sentido que los/las 14-15 docentes le dan a la IA generativa como herramienta de diseño didáctico, no un artefacto de la interpretación de la investigadora.
- **Operationalization:**
  - Triangulación entre los cuatro ejes (A-D, §1) dentro de la misma entrevista — ¿el mismo/a docente es consistente entre lo que dice sobre sus competencias digitales (Eje A) y lo que hace/imagina hacer con IAG (Eje D)?
  - Peer debriefing: al menos una sesión documentada (fecha, cambios resultantes) con el/la director/a de tesis revisando el codebook y las categorías axiales emergentes.
  - Member checking parcial y factible: síntesis de una página por participante, con sus propios fragmentos codificados y la interpretación asignada, enviada para confirmación/corrección — factible dado N=14-15.
  - Análisis de casos negativos/desviantes para cada categoría axial propuesta (ver Robustness Plan).

### Transferability
- **Statement:** Los lectores pueden juzgar a qué otros contextos se transfieren estos hallazgos, sin reclamo de representatividad estadística.
- **Operationalization:** Tabla de descripción densa de los 14-15 participantes — años de antigüedad, área curricular, grado/ciclo, tipo de gestión (pública/privada), exposición previa declarada a IAG. Declaración explícita: "los hallazgos se transfieren a docentes con perfiles y contextos institucionales similares a los descritos; no se reclama representatividad del universo de docentes de nivel primario de CABA" (ver §4, §6.1).

### Dependability
- **Statement:** El proceso de análisis es sistemático, documentado, auditable.
- **Operationalization:** Historial de versiones del codebook con fecha y justificación de cada cambio (incluyendo el flag `[deductivo]`/`[inductivo]` de §2.3); protocolo de entrevista incluido como apéndice, con nota explícita de qué bloques fueron efectivamente administrados a cada participante; bitácora de memos analíticos.

### Confirmability
- **Statement:** Los hallazgos están anclados en los datos, no solo en las predisposiciones de la investigadora.
- **Operationalization:** Cita textual (verbatim, en cursiva) de al menos un fragmento por cada categoría axial reportada; auditoría parcial de codificación (2-3 de 14-15 entrevistas revisadas por un segundo lector); reflexividad explícita (ver abajo).

### Theoretical Saturation at N=14-15 (not code saturation alone)

GT's rigor standard is **theoretical saturation** — the point at which new data no longer reveals new properties of the core categories or their relationships — which is a stronger, more specific claim than mere "code saturation" (no new labels appearing). Operationalize both, and be honest about which was reached:

- **Code-level saturation:** cumulative new-codes-per-interview curve (tally codes first appearing at each successive interview, in the order interviews were coded) — report whether it flattens before interview 14-15.
- **Theoretical saturation:** for each proposed axial/central category, explicitly ask and answer: "did later interviews add new *properties or relationships* to this category (not just new instances confirming it)?" If the answer becomes consistently "no" for the last 2-3 interviews across all axial categories, report theoretical saturation as reached. **If it is not clearly reached** (plausible at N=14-15 for a category this rich — four theoretical núcleos plus an imaginaries dimension is a lot of conceptual ground for 14-15 interviews), state this honestly as a scope limitation on the substantive theory produced, rather than claiming a "complete" account.

### Reflexivity Plan — including the researcher's own prior publication

This is a genuine reflexivity issue, not a formality, and must be treated as such:

1. **Declaración explícita de la relación con el objeto de estudio previo:** la investigadora es coautora de Caldeiro, Odetti y Ordiz (2024), el primer estudio empírico identificado sobre IAG en educación primaria porteña. Antes de comenzar la codificación formal, debe redactar un memo reflexivo respondiendo explícitamente: **¿qué espera encontrar en este corpus, dado lo que ya observó/concluyó en el estudio de 2024? ¿Qué categorías o patrones podría estar "reconociendo" en lugar de genuinamente descubriendo, por haber trabajado ya este tema?** This is precisely the risk GT's constant-comparison and negative-case-search methods are designed to guard against (§2.3), but the guard only works if the expectation is named explicitly first.
2. **Postura personal frente a IAG** (uso propio, entusiasmo/escepticismo) y vínculo institucional con las escuelas de acceso (relevante dado el elemento de conveniencia en la ampliación muestral, §4).
3. **Relación previa con los/las docentes entrevistados/as** (colegas, conocidos, contactos de la expansión por conveniencia) — relevante tanto para deseabilidad social (§6.3) como para credibilidad.
4. Esta declaración se **actualiza como memo continuo** durante la codificación, no se escribe una sola vez al inicio — cada vez que un hallazgo "confirma" algo esperado del estudio de 2024, registrar explícitamente esa coincidencia y si se buscó activamente un caso que la contradijera.

## 4. Sample Handling — honest documentation of the real sampling history

**Original design (documented in the thesis plan, p. 12):** N=10, muestreo intencional, con criterios explícitos de heterogeneidad: (a) nivel de familiaridad con herramientas digitales, (b) área curricular, (c) tipo de gestión escolar (pública/privada).

**What actually happened:** The committee requested an expansion to a target of N=15; **14-15 interviews have been completed** at the time of this memo. The incorporation of the additional ~5 cases beyond the original 10 involved, per domain-profile.md, **a practical/access-based (convenience) element layered on top of the original intentional design logic** — i.e., not every additional case was selected purely to fill a specific gap in the original three-criteria matrix; some were added because access was practically available.

**How this memo recommends handling it (do not overclaim, do not underclaim):**
- Report **both** components explicitly and separately in the methods section: "el diseño muestral original (N=10) fue intencional, según tres criterios [...]; la ampliación a 15 casos, solicitada por el comité, incorporó estos criterios en la medida de lo posible, con un componente adicional de acceso práctico en la selección de los casos incorporados en la segunda etapa."
- **Do not** claim the full N=14-15 sample was selected by the original three-criteria design — this would overclaim intentionality that was not, in practice, fully sustained.
- **Do not** dismiss the original design logic either — the original 10 cases genuinely were selected against explicit heterogeneity criteria, and this partial intentionality is a real methodological asset worth reporting (it is stronger than a purely convenience sample from the start).
- Populate the thick-description table (§3, Transferability) with a column flagging which cases came from the original intentional wave vs. the expansion wave, so a reader can see exactly where the heterogeneity claim is strongest and where it is weakest.
- This is precisely the kind of sampling evolution that referees ask about directly (domain-profile.md, Field-Specific Referee Concerns: "Tamaño y composición de la muestra, y su evolución de 10 a 15") — pre-empted honestly here rather than glossed over.

## 6. Threats / Limitations (addressed explicitly, not papered over)

### 6.1 Semi-structured coverage variability — frequency claims must be conditioned

The protocol was **not administered uniformly** across the 14-15 participants (explicit warning already present in the protocol document itself). This has a direct, non-optional consequence: **any claim of the form "most/several/few teachers mentioned X" must be conditioned on the actual denominator of participants for whom the relevant block/question was administered, not on the full N=14-15.** A theme's apparent absence in a given interview may reflect that the corresponding block simply was not asked, not that the teacher genuinely lacks that element of their representation. **Concrete implementation:** the coding scheme (§2.6) must carry a per-segment flag recording which protocol block/question the segment responds to, so that every reported frequency in the final paper can be reported as "n/m, where m = number of participants for whom this block was administered," not simply "n/14-15."

### 6.2 The aula/tareas_docentes split — explicit recommendation: DROP

The superseded memo invented a domain split (`aula` vs. `tareas_docentes`) treating classroom/student-facing use and the teacher's own preparation work as two "campos de práctica" of a single representational object, and built an entire empirical question (stable vs. fragmented núcleo across domains) around it. **This memo recommends dropping that split entirely**, for three convergent reasons:

1. **Not in the source document.** Neither the thesis plan (RQ, sub-questions, 5 objetivos específicos, marco teórico, estrategia metodológica) nor the Ateneo presentation (per domain-profile's summary) proposes this split anywhere. It was invented by the earlier, incorrect analysis.
2. **Not supported by the protocol.** Block 6 ("usos proyectados en propuestas didácticas") is squarely about the teacher's own planning/preparation work. Blocks 4-5 concern general concerns/beliefs/pedagogical theory. **No block directly asks about in-classroom, student-facing AI use** (e.g., nothing like "¿cómo usarías IAG junto con tus estudiantes, en tiempo real, durante la clase?"). Attempting to code and report an "aula" domain with this thin, indirect coverage would produce a badly asymmetric, unreliable comparison — precisely the kind of artifact §6.1 warns about, compounded.
3. **The real object is already single and well-specified.** The RQ and objetivos específicos are about IAG's potential for meaningful learning *via didactic proposals the teacher designs* — this is inherently the teacher's own planning/preparation work (Axis D, §1), not a separate "aula" object requiring its own domain tag. Replacing the invented split with the four real axes (A-D, §1) gives the analysis a sharper, better-evidenced structure without losing anything the thesis plan actually asks for.

**If the researcher wants to say something about in-classroom use anyway:** any such comments that do surface (teachers sometimes volunteer classroom scenarios unprompted, e.g., in Bloque 7's "escenarios futuros") can still be coded and reported as illustrative material under the relevant axis (typically D) — just not elevated into a structural, symmetric domain comparison the data cannot support.

### 6.3 Social-desirability bias — no indirect questions available

Teachers may minimize rejection of, or unfamiliarity with, generative AI in front of the interviewer. Unlike some SRT designs, **this protocol contains no indirect/projective questions** (nothing like "¿qué dirían tus colegas...?") — that particular mitigation tactic is simply unavailable here; it should not be listed as a planned mitigation (as it was, hypothetically, flagged for checking in the superseded memo) since the protocol has been directly verified not to contain it. Available mitigations instead:
- Declare the limitation explicitly in the paper's limitations section, without pretending it has been eliminated.
- **Weight concrete, specific episodes over general declarative statements** when identifying axial-category evidence — general claims of enthusiasm/competence are more vulnerable to desirability effects than specific, recent, detailed anecdotes a teacher volunteers.
- Use the objetivación anchor (§2.5) as a partial cross-check: a teacher's spontaneous first metaphor for IAG is harder to strategically manage than a direct yes/no answer about, say, whether they use it responsibly.

### 6.4 Scooping-risk caveat — novelty claim is provisional, not settled

Per the corrected `positioning.md` and the addendum literature review, the claim that no existing study combines imaginarios sociotécnicos + docentes + IA generativa + nivel primario rests on two literature searches, **but two of the closest-overlap candidates were only reachable via blocked (403) or unconfirmed-authorship sources**:
1. *"Sociotechnical Imaginaries in a Postdigital World: Teachers' Perceptions of Digital Citizenship Education"* (Postdigital Science and Education, 2025) — combines imaginarios + percepciones docentes explicitly in the title; access blocked; object (digital citizenship, not generative AI specifically) suggests medium risk, not direct competition, but this is unconfirmed.
2. An unidentified Chilean article in REEC 48 (2025) on imaginarios sociotécnicos and "alienación docente" — authorship unconfirmed; object (TIC/transformación digital in general vs. generative AI specifically) suggests medium-low risk, but likewise unconfirmed.

**This memo does not treat the novelty claim as settled.** The positioning language used in the eventual paper should be hedged accordingly ("hasta donde pudo determinarse en dos rondas de búsqueda bibliográfica..." — language already used in positioning.md's own draft contribution statement) rather than an unqualified "first study to..." claim, until these two sources are verified. **Recommend a targeted follow-up search/access attempt before the writer phase locks in final positioning language.**

### 6.5 Imaginaries-as-active-dimension — risk of over-inferring macro from micro (see also §7, objection new-5)

Already addressed mechanically in §2.4, Step 4: the design explicitly limits imaginaries claims to echo/departure from a specific, light reference-corpus marker list, not a freestanding characterization of "the" national imaginary. Restated here as a limitation: because the Guía 2025 and Educ.ar survey are read once and not exhaustively coded, the marker list itself may be incomplete or may miss shifts in official discourse not captured in these two documents. This is a bounded-scope decision (appropriate for a master's thesis), not an oversight, but it should be named as a limitation, not silently assumed away.

### 6.6 Encuesta component — internal tension in the source document, unresolved here

As noted in the Pre-Strategy Report, the thesis plan's introduction mentions a mixed encuesta + entrevista design, but its own methodology section never operationalizes a survey. This memo proceeds on interviews as the sole primary data source (consistent with domain-profile.md's current data inventory) but flags this tension for the researcher to resolve explicitly — if survey data does exist, the data-assessment picture and possibly this memo's design would need updating.

3. **"Tamaño y composición de la muestra, y su evolución de 10 a 15"** (domain-profile.md)
   - **Response:** Full honest disclosure per §4 — original intentional N=10 with named criteria, committee-requested expansion to 14-15 with a documented convenience element in the second wave, thick-description table flagging which wave each case came from.

5. **[NEW — replaces the discarded Abric-specific objection] "¿Cómo se justifica tratar los imaginarios sociotécnicos como una dimensión rastreada en el discurso docente, y no solo como marco teórico de fondo — y no hay riesgo de sobre-inferir el nivel macro (el imaginario nacional/oficial) a partir de un corpus de entrevistas de nivel micro, sin un análisis documental exhaustivo paralelo?"** (domain-profile.md, Field-Specific Referee Concerns — this is the exact tension the researcher herself raised in the Ateneo)
